# Content quality and helpfulness

Use this when reviewing or writing page copy for search. Align with Google's
people-first content guidance. Do not invent E-E-A-T scores or ranking weights.

## People-first checks

Ask of every page:

- Does it provide original information, experience, or analysis?
- Is the topic covered substantially, or is it thin / incomplete?
- Would a reader bookmark, share, or cite it?
- Does the main heading summarize the page without sensational exaggeration?
- Is it well produced (clear structure, accurate facts, few typos)?
- Does it avoid merely rewriting other sources with no added value?

## Search intent

| Intent | Visitor wants | Page should |
|--------|---------------|-------------|
| Informational | Learn / understand | Clear answer early, structured explanation |
| Navigational | Reach a known destination | Brand/product clarity, obvious path |
| Commercial investigation | Compare options | Criteria, tradeoffs, honest limits |
| Transactional | Complete an action | Direct CTA, minimal friction, trust signals |

Mismatch between query intent and page purpose is a common reason strong
keyword targeting still fails.

## Trust signals (especially YMYL)

For topics that affect health, money, safety, or major life decisions:

- Prefer accurate, expert-reviewed claims over marketing speculation
- Show who wrote or published the content when authorship matters
- Cite primary sources; date-stamp time-sensitive advice
- Avoid medical, legal, or financial guarantees in copy

Trust matters on non-YMYL pages too: accurate product claims, working links,
and transparent pricing or limitations all help.

## On-page substance

- Lead with the answer or value, then supporting detail
- Use descriptive subheads that could stand alone in a snippet
- Prefer concrete examples, steps, and constraints over vague adjectives
- Keep one primary topic per URL; split unrelated subjects
- Update or retire outdated pages instead of leaving contradictory versions live

## What not to do

- Keyword stuffing or unnatural synonym repetition
- Doorway pages built only to rank for slight keyword variants
- Copied or spun content scaled across many URLs
- Engagement bait titles that do not match the body
- Auto-generated bulk pages with no editorial care
